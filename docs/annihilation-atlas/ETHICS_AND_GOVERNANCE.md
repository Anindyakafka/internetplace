# Ethics and Governance

## Non-negotiable commitments

Annihilation Atlas is against caste and jati hierarchy. It does not treat caste as a cultural taxonomy to preserve, rank, predict, or browse. It studies the institutions and material relations through which domination is reproduced and the movements through which it is resisted.

The project will not publish:

- Caste or jati prediction from names, language, appearance, location, occupation, or networks.
- Searchable individual records or household-level maps.
- Exact locations of vulnerable settlements, incidents, interviewees, or private archives when disclosure creates risk.
- Rankings of communities or claims of inherent group characteristics.
- Administrative categories presented as timeless identities.
- Sensitive field material without informed, specific, revocable permission.
- Small cells whose combination with other public sources could identify people.

## Evidence labels

Every claim must distinguish among:

- **Self-described** — terminology chosen by the person, movement, or community in the cited context.
- **Administratively classified** — category assigned through a state schedule, census, survey, court, or programme.
- **Researcher-coded** — category constructed during analysis; coding rules must be public.
- **Historically attributed** — term used by a historical source and not adopted as present-day truth.
- **Contested** — classification or interpretation challenged in the archive.

## Publication levels

| Level | Material | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Official aggregate tables, public documents, approved derived aggregates | May be versioned in the site repository |
| Controlled | Licensed microdata, unsuppressed small cells, detailed working geography | Local analysis only; publish reviewed aggregates |
| Private | Interviews, personal information, exact sensitive locations, unpublished archives | Encrypted/restricted; never committed to Git |
| Prohibited | Data intended for caste inference, ranking, targeting, or surveillance | Do not collect or derive |

## Review gates

No visualization moves to the public site until it passes:

1. **Source review** — provenance and licence are complete.
2. **Method review** — denominator, weights, joins, missingness, and uncertainty are documented.
3. **Political review** — framing does not naturalize hierarchy or erase resistance.
4. **Disclosure review** — re-identification and targeting risks are acceptable.
5. **Accessibility review** — the argument does not depend only on colour, motion, pointer interaction, or visual acuity.

## Objections and corrections

- Factual corrections receive a dated public record.
- Requests involving safety or personal information are handled before ordinary editorial disputes.
- A person who contributed an interview or private item may request withdrawal according to the consent agreement.
- Community objections are recorded with the disputed claim even where interpretive disagreement remains unresolved.
- Removal from the public interface does not imply silent deletion from the research audit trail; the private record should state what changed and why.

## Language

- Prefer the terminology used by anti-caste movements and the cited people in context.
- Preserve harmful historical terms only where necessary for analysis; label and contextualize them.
- Do not use euphemisms such as “traditional occupation” where coercion, exclusion, or hereditary labour is at issue.
- Do not make oppressed communities visible only through deprivation or violence.
- Translate local terms with notes rather than forcing false English equivalence.
