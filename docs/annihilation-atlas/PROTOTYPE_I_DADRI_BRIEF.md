# Prototype I: Dadri

## Working title

**Dadri: What the Map Cannot Say**

## Position

The first Annihilation Atlas investigation begins in Dadri, Gautam Buddha Nagar. It grows from Anindya's participation in the Dadri Forecast collective and from the relationships, field practices, questions, and counter-public work developed around it. The Atlas may cite and respond to Dadri Forecast, but it must not imply that it speaks for every member of the collective or for the people of Dadri.

This is an explicitly anti-caste, anti-jati, minority-aligned, and Marxian project. It studies caste and class together without reducing either to the other. It treats communalism, property, labour, ecology, state power, and accumulation as connected relations rather than separate themes.

## Central question

> How have changing regimes of land, labour, infrastructure, classification, and communal power remade Dadri, and how have caste-oppressed, minoritised, working, agrarian, and displaced communities lived, contested, and exceeded that transformation?

The question is a research direction, not a conclusion. Every public claim must be supported, bounded, and open to correction.

## Scope: "everything" as a relational system

"Everything" will not become one totalising index or a dashboard that ranks places and communities. The prototype will connect seven domains:

1. **Land, property, and commons** - tenure, acquisition, compensation, land-use change, common land, wetlands, and displacement.
2. **Labour and class** - agrarian labour, industrial work, informal work, migration, wages, occupational change, and social reproduction.
3. **Caste and jati power** - segregation, occupation, access, violence, state classification, everyday reproduction, and anti-caste refusal.
4. **Communalism and minority life** - institutions, spatial stigma, violence, policing, memory, survival, and solidarity, without mapping vulnerable households.
5. **Ecology** - water, wetlands, air, soil, extraction, waste, industrial pollution, and unequal exposure.
6. **Development, state, and capital** - planning, infrastructure, industry, real estate, acquisition, courts, bureaucracy, and the production of "surplus" populations.
7. **Resistance, care, and counter-knowledge** - organising, art, education, play, oral history, collective memory, mutual care, and Dadri Forecast's practices.

## Historical transition

The study will connect the contemporary moment to a longer transition. Period boundaries will be set only after the source audit, but the working sequence is:

- earlier settlement, agrarian, water, and commons histories;
- colonial revenue, settlement, transport, and administrative classification;
- post-independence agrarian and institutional change;
- industrial, power, and infrastructure expansion;
- NCR planning, district reorganisation, land acquisition, and peri-urban real-estate growth;
- communalisation and the political life of violence and memory;
- contemporary ecological crisis, dispossession, collective practice, and resistance.

No period will be presented as a harmonious caste-free past. Colonial records will be read as instruments of rule as well as historical evidence.

## Two-voice editorial architecture

### Situated voice

First-person passages may describe Anindya's encounters, uncertainty, participation in Dadri Forecast, field methods, and changes in understanding. These passages must distinguish direct observation, recollection, interpretation, and information received from others.

### Research voice

Institutional prose will present sourced claims, methods, definitions, denominators, disputes, and limitations. It will not borrow the authority of the first-person voice to make unsupported generalisations.

The interface must label the voices clearly. Oral histories and community testimony remain their speakers' knowledge and require consent; they are not raw material for an omniscient narrator.

## Five rooms

1. **The Map** - changing boundaries, land use, commons, infrastructure, industry, water, and selected aggregate social conditions. Historical layers and boundary uncertainty remain visible.
2. **The Measure** - linked evidence across the seven domains, with no composite caste score and no individual lookup.
3. **The File** - revenue records, plans, censuses, acquisition notices, environmental documents, court records, and the history of the categories they produce.
4. **The Absence** - missing jati data, incompatible boundaries, displaced people omitted by enumeration, unrecorded labour, unreported violence, inaccessible archives, and information withheld for safety.
5. **The Struggle** - Dadri Forecast, Khandera Art Space, movements, workers, residents, educators, artists, children, and other forms of organising and collective care, subject to permission and plurality of account.

## First investigable threads

- What did land acquisition and land-use change redistribute, from whom, to whom, and through which institutions?
- How did agrarian, industrial, informal, and reproductive labour change alongside urbanisation?
- Where do official caste categories reveal inequality, and where do they erase jati, religion, gender, migration, or informal settlement?
- How are pollution, depleted water, wetland loss, and infrastructural burdens socially distributed?
- How have communal violence and its administrative narration reshaped public space and minority life?
- What practices of resistance, education, care, art, and memory produce another account of Dadri?

## Publication limits

- No surname-to-caste inference, caste ranking, or identification of individuals.
- No exact locations of vulnerable households, interviewees, or sensitive sites.
- No publication of land-record names or personal compensation details merely because they are accessible.
- No small-cell map that enables re-identification.
- No testimony, photograph, audio, or Dadri Forecast material without appropriate permission.
- No unverified fieldwork number or methodology claim. Existing website copy is a lead, not evidence.

## First release test

Prototype I is ready only when each mapped or written claim has a source, time period, unit, category-maker, limitation, and counter-account; the five rooms are substantively connected; Dadri Forecast's relationship to the work is reviewed; and political, privacy, factual, and accessibility reviews are complete.
